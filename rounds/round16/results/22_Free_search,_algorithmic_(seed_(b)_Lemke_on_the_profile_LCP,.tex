\subsection{Lemke's algorithm is a bias homotopy}\label{sec:fsa-lemke}

\Cref{rem:lcp} closes the \emph{interior-point} route through the
complementarity form of \Cref{prop:lcp}: the handicap of a sufficient matrix
can be exponential, so those iteration bounds are not polynomial. It says
nothing about the \emph{pivoting} route, and Lemke's algorithm terminates on
every $P$-matrix linear complementarity problem, so it is a complete
algorithm for \Cref{prob:main} of a shape that no barrier of \Cref{sec:gap}
covers. This subsection says what Lemke's algorithm does on a simple
stochastic game, in the language of the game rather than of the tableau, and
measures it.

\begin{definition}[The biased system]\label{fsa:def-bias}
Let $G$ be stopping in harmonic normal form on $C=\Vmax\cup\Vmin$
(\Cref{def:profile-orientation}), with readouts $f_{v,a}$ and signs
$\varepsilon_v$. Fix a \emph{base profile} $\beta\in\{0,1\}^{C}$ and a
positive \emph{covering vector} $d\in\mathbb R^{C}_{>0}$. For $\lambda\ge0$
put
\[
  f^{\lambda}_{v,a}(y)\;:=\;f_{v,a}(y)-\varepsilon_v d_v\lambda\,
     [\,a\ne\beta(v)\,],
\]
so that at every controlled vertex the base action is favoured by $d_v\lambda$
against its owner's alternative, and let
$T_\lambda(y)_v:=\operatorname{opt}_a f^{\lambda}_{v,a}(y)$ be the
corresponding operator on $\mathbb R^{C}$. Call a profile $\pi$
\emph{$\lambda$-optimal} if $y_\pi$, the unique solution of
$y_v=f^{\lambda}_{v,\pi(v)}(y)$, satisfies $T_\lambda y_\pi=y_\pi$.
By \Cref{lem:survival-contract} the operator $T_\lambda$ has exactly one
fixed point $y(\lambda)$ for every $\lambda$, the rewards being arbitrary
reals there, and $y(0)=\val|_C$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Lemke's algorithm is the bias homotopy]\label{fsa:lemke-homotopy}
Let $G$ be stopping, $\beta$, $d$ as above, and let $M,q$ be the data of
\Cref{prop:lcp} for the base $\beta$; write $z,w$ for its variables. Then
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=2.2em]
\item for every $\lambda\ge0$ the pair $(z,w+\lambda d)$ solves
  $\mathrm{LCP}(M,q+\lambda d)$ if and only if the underlying $y$ is
  $y(\lambda)$; the solution is unique, $M$ being a $P$-matrix, so
  $\lambda\mapsto z(\lambda)$ is a well-defined piecewise affine path;
\item $\lambda_0:=\max_v\max\bigl(0,-q_v/d_v\bigr)$ is the least $\lambda$ at
  which $\beta$ is $\lambda$-optimal, and $\beta$ is $\lambda$-optimal for
  every $\lambda\ge\lambda_0$;
\item for $S\subseteq C$ the set $I_S$ of $\lambda\ge0$ whose solution has
  $z_v=0$ off $S$ and $(w+\lambda d)_v=0$ on $S$ is a closed interval, on
  which $z_S(\lambda)=-(M_{SS})^{-1}(q_S+\lambda d_S)$ is affine; the $I_S$
  cover $[0,\infty)$ and meet only in endpoints, so
  $\lambda\mapsto S(\lambda)$ is a step function with at most $2^{|C|}-1$
  breakpoints and pairwise distinct values, and the corresponding profiles
  $\beta\oplus S$ form a self-avoiding path in the profile cube;
\item Lemke's algorithm with covering vector $d$, started from the initial
  ray $z=0$, traverses exactly
  $\{(z(\lambda),w(\lambda)+\lambda d,\lambda):0\le\lambda\le\lambda_0\}$
  with $\lambda$ decreasing, and terminates at $\lambda=0$ with
  $y(0)=\val|_C$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(a) Write $w^{\lambda}:=w+\lambda d$. By \Cref{prop:lcp},
$z_v=\varepsilon_v(y_v-f_{v,\beta(v)}(y))=
\varepsilon_v(y_v-f^{\lambda}_{v,\beta(v)}(y))$ and
$w^{\lambda}_v=\varepsilon_v(y_v-f_{v,\bar\beta(v)}(y))+\lambda d_v
=\varepsilon_v(y_v-f^{\lambda}_{v,\bar\beta(v)}(y))$. Hence
$z,w^\lambda\ge0$ says that at a Max vertex $y_v$ dominates both biased
readouts and at a Min vertex is dominated by both, while
$z^{\mathsf T}w^{\lambda}=0$ says one of the two is attained at each vertex;
together these are $T_\lambda y=y$, whose solution is unique
(\Cref{lem:survival-contract}). Since $y\mapsto z$ is an affine bijection and
$w=Mz+q$, these conditions are $\mathrm{LCP}(M,q+\lambda d)$, and $M$ is a
$P$-matrix by \Cref{prop:lcp}(a).

(b) At $z=0$ the underlying $y$ is $y_\beta$ (\Cref{prop:lcp}(b) with
$S=\emptyset$) and $w^{\lambda}=q+\lambda d$, a solution iff
$q+\lambda d\ge0$.

(c) On $I_S$ the equations $w^{\lambda}_S=0$ read
$M_{SS}z_S+q_S+\lambda d_S=0$, determining $z_S$ affinely --- $M_{SS}$ is a
$P$-matrix, hence invertible --- and $I_S$ is cut out by the affine
inequalities $z_S(\lambda)\ge0$ and
$(Mz(\lambda)+q+\lambda d)_{C\setminus S}\ge0$, so it is an interval.
Uniqueness in (a) makes the interiors of the $I_S$ disjoint with union
$[0,\infty)$, so each $S$ occurs on one interval only.

(d) Along an edge of Lemke's almost-complementary path the basis is
$\{z_v:v\in S\}\cup\{w_v:v\notin S\}\cup\{z_0\}$ and one complementary pair
is nonbasic, so every point of the edge is a \emph{complementary} solution of
$\mathrm{LCP}(M,q+\lambda d)$ for $\lambda=z_0$; by (a) the edge lies in the
graph of the function $\lambda\mapsto z(\lambda)$, so the path projects
injectively to $\lambda$. It starts at $\lambda_0$ and, secondary ray
termination being impossible for a $P$-matrix, ends at $\lambda=0$; being
connected it is the whole segment.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The homotopy is an exact algorithm]\label{fsa:algorithm}
On a stopping SSG the \emph{bias homotopy} --- start at $\pi_0=\beta$,
$\lambda=\lambda_0$; on the current piece solve
$y(\lambda)=\alpha+\lambda\gamma$ by two exact $|C|\times|C|$ linear systems;
take the largest $\lambda'<\lambda$ at which some regret
$\rho_v(\lambda):=\varepsilon_v\bigl(y_v(\lambda)-
f^{\lambda}_{v,\bar\pi(v)}(y(\lambda))\bigr)$ vanishes; switch that $v$ and
repeat --- terminates with $\val|_C$ after at most $2^{|C|}-1$ switches, each
costing $O(|C|^{3})$ exact rational operations on numbers of polynomial
bit-size, and decides \Cref{prob:main}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}\label{fsa:attribution}
The reduction of turn-based stochastic games to $P$-matrix LCPs and the
Stickney--Watson correspondence are attributed in \Cref{rem:lcp,prop:lcp}
(G\"artner--R\"ust; Jurdzi\'nski--Savani; Hansen--Ibsen-Jensen). That the
Lemke path of a $P$-matrix LCP is the solution path of the one-parameter
family $\mathrm{LCP}(M,q+\lambda d)$ is standard in the complementarity
literature (Lemke; Cottle--Pang--Stone), reproved above only because the
exact form is needed; from memory, unchecked. What appears new here is the
reading of $\lambda$ as a \emph{loyalty bonus} paid to the base profile,
\Cref{fsa:one-improving,fsa:path-lp} and the measurements.
\end{remark}

\subsection{What the homotopy does}\label{sec:fsa-what}

\begin{proposition}[One player: an improving single-switch rule]\label{fsa:one-improving}
Let $G$ be a stopping SSG with $\Vmin=\emptyset$, let
$\mu_1>\mu_2>\dots>\mu_K>\mu_{K+1}:=0$ be the breakpoints of the bias homotopy
and $\pi_0=\beta,\pi_1,\dots,\pi_K$ its profiles, so that $\pi_0$ is
$\lambda$-optimal on $[\mu_1,\infty)$ and $\pi_t$ on $[\mu_{t+1},\mu_t]$ for
$1\le t\le K$ (all these intervals nondegenerate --- if two breakpoints
coincide, delete the intermediate profile and the statement is unchanged).
Write $\kappa_\pi(v):=\sum_{w}d_w\,\mathbb E_\pi[N_w\mid v]\,[\pi(w)\ne\beta(w)]$
for the $d$-weighted expected number of visits to the deviating vertices.
Then for every $0\le t<K$,
\[
  \val_{\pi_{t+1}}-\val_{\pi_t}\;=\;\mu_{t+1}\bigl(\kappa_{\pi_{t+1}}-\kappa_{\pi_t}\bigr)\;\ge\;0
  \qquad\text{pointwise,}
\]
so the path is a nondecreasing chain of value vectors and, consecutive
profiles differing in one coordinate, every step is an improving single
switch: on one-player games the bias homotopy is a switching rule in the
sense of \Cref{def:rule}, and one not among the seven of
\Cref{prop:rules-fail}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
All $\varepsilon_v=+1$, so the biased value of a fixed profile is
$\val^{\lambda}_\pi=\val_\pi-\lambda\kappa_\pi$: the bias subtracts
$d_w\lambda$ at each visit to a deviating $w$. At $\lambda=\mu_{t+1}$ both
$\pi_t$ and $\pi_{t+1}$ are $\lambda$-optimal, hence both attain the optimal
value vector of that one-player game, giving
$\Delta v=\mu_{t+1}\Delta\kappa$ with
$\Delta v:=\val_{\pi_{t+1}}-\val_{\pi_t}$,
$\Delta\kappa:=\kappa_{\pi_{t+1}}-\kappa_{\pi_t}$. For
$\lambda\in[\mu_{t+2},\mu_{t+1})$, a nondegenerate interval, the profile
$\pi_{t+1}$ is optimal, so $\Delta v-\lambda\Delta\kappa\ge0$; substituting
the identity gives $(\mu_{t+1}-\lambda)\Delta\kappa\ge0$, hence
$\Delta\kappa\ge0$ and $\Delta v\ge0$. A single switch that does not lower the value is an improving
switch.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[One player: the path is a shadow-vertex path]\label{fsa:shadow}
Let $G$ be a stopping SSG with $\Vmin=\emptyset$ and harmonic normal form
$(q^{v,a},p^{v,a})$ on $\Vmax$, $|\Vmax|=m$, and let
$\mathrm{Occ}(\mu)=\{f\ge0:\ \sum_{a}f_{v,a}-\sum_{u,a}f_{u,a}p^{u,a}_v=\mu_v\}$
be d'Epenoux's occupancy polytope for an initial distribution $\mu>0$. It is
a simple $m$-polytope with $2m$ facets whose $2^{m}$ vertices are the
deterministic profiles, and $\pi$ is $\lambda$-optimal exactly when $f^{\pi}$
maximises $(c-\lambda e_\beta)\cdot f$ over $\mathrm{Occ}(\mu)$, where
$c_{v,a}:=q^{v,a}$ and $(e_\beta)_{v,a}:=d_v[a\ne\beta(v)]$. Hence the bias
homotopy is the parametric --- equivalently shadow-vertex --- simplex path of
that linear programme in the $2$-plane spanned by $c$ and $e_\beta$, started
at the vertex $f^{\beta}$, and its length is the number of vertices of the
shadow of $\mathrm{Occ}(\mu)$ in that plane between $f^{\beta}$ and the
optimum.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The occupancy correspondence and the identification of vertices with
deterministic profiles is d'Epenoux's, already used in
\Cref{prop:oneplayer-lp}; $\lambda$-optimality of $\pi$ is optimality of the
policy in the one-player game with rewards $c-\lambda e_\beta$, which for
$\mu>0$ is optimality of $f^{\pi}$ in the linear programme.
\end{proof}

So for one player the question ``how long is Lemke's path on a simple
stochastic game'' is ``how long can the shadow of the occupancy polytope of a
two-action Markov decision process be'', with the projection direction
constrained to weight one action-coordinate per state. That $d$-polytopes
with $2d$ facets can have $2^{\Omega(d)}$-vertex shadows is Amenta and
Ziegler's (deformed products and maximal shadows), after Klee--Minty, Murty
and Goldfarb; that parametric shortest paths --- the special case in which
every occupancy is $0$ or $1$ --- have $n^{\Theta(\log n)}$ breakpoints is
Carstensen's. Both from memory, unchecked. Neither construction is an
occupancy polytope of the shape above and we could not turn either into one.

\begin{proposition}[Two players: not strategy improvement]\label{fsa:two-nonmono}
The identity $\val_{\pi_{t+1}}-\val_{\pi_t}=\mu_{t+1}
(\kappa^{\varepsilon}_{\pi_{t+1}}-\kappa^{\varepsilon}_{\pi_t})$, with
$\kappa^{\varepsilon}_\pi(v)=\sum_w\varepsilon_wd_w\mathbb E_\pi[N_w\mid v]
[\pi(w)\ne\beta(w)]$, holds on every stopping SSG by the same proof, but its
right-hand side has no sign when both players are present. Measured, in exact
arithmetic: the identity held at all $303$ homotopy steps drawn from random
stopping games on $10$ vertices and at all $248$ steps of an independent
re-run on $13$-vertex games at a fresh seed; of the $181+137$ two-player
steps, $99+66$ \emph{lowered} some coordinate of $\val_\pi$, while all
$122+111$ one-player steps raised none and were improving single switches. So on two-player games the
homotopy is not a switching rule of \Cref{def:rule}, and neither
\Cref{cor:no-height,thm:impedance,thm:normalform-barrier,thm:window-barrier}
nor \Cref{thm:matching-barrier,thm:slack-barrier,thm:seeded-barrier,thm:ratio-sandwich}
speaks about it; it is also not residue-blind, reading as it does the whole
harmonic normal form.
\end{proposition}

\begin{lemma}[Realising a prescribed path is one linear programme]\label{fsa:path-lp}
Fix the transition part $(Q_\beta,Q_{\bar\beta})$ of a harmonic normal form on
$C$, the signs $\varepsilon$ and the covering vector $d$; then
$M=E(I-Q_{\bar\beta})(I-Q_\beta)^{-1}E$ is fixed and
$q=MEr_\beta-Er_{\bar\beta}$ is affine in the reward vectors. Let
$S_0=\emptyset,S_1,\dots,S_K$ be subsets of $C$ with
$|S_t\triangle S_{t-1}|=1$. Then the reward vectors and breakpoints
$\lambda_1>\dots>\lambda_K>0$ for which the bias homotopy has exactly that
sequence of supports are the solutions of the linear system
\[
  0\le r_{\beta}\le\bbone-Q_\beta\bbone,\quad
  0\le r_{\bar\beta}\le\bbone-Q_{\bar\beta}\bbone,\quad
  q+\lambda_1d\ge0,
\]
\[
  z_{S_t}(\lambda)\ge0\ \text{and}\ \bigl(Mz(\lambda)+q+\lambda d\bigr)_i\ge0\ (i\notin S_t)
  \quad\text{at }\lambda=\lambda_t\text{ and }\lambda=\lambda_{t+1},\ 1\le t\le K,
\]
with $\lambda_{K+1}:=0$ and $z_{S}(\lambda)=-(M_{SS})^{-1}(q_S+\lambda d_S)$,
all of whose left-hand sides are affine in
$(r_\beta,r_{\bar\beta},\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_K)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$z_S$ and $w$ are affine in $(q,\lambda)$ by
\Cref{fsa:lemke-homotopy}(c), and $q$ is affine in the rewards by
\Cref{prop:lcp}; an affine function of $\lambda$ nonnegative at both
endpoints of an interval is nonnegative on it; and by uniqueness of the
solution, ``$z_{S}(\lambda)\ge0$ and $w_i(\lambda)\ge0$ off $S$'' says exactly
that the solution at $\lambda$ is the complementary basic solution with basis
$S$, i.e.\ that the profile there is $\beta\oplus S$. The reward bounds are
the constraints $q^{v,a}\ge0$, $q^{v,a}+\sum_wp^{v,a}_w\le1$ of a harmonic
normal form.
\end{proof}

This is what makes search possible: the rewards, which are where all the
freedom sits, are eliminated by a linear programme, so only the transition
structure has to be searched, and for a fixed structure the \emph{longest}
realisable path is found by a depth-first search over paths in the profile
cube with one linear program per extension. All the witnesses below were found
that way and then rounded to dyadics and re-verified exactly from the game.

\begin{proposition}[The named families do not defeat the homotopy]\label{fsa:families}
Let $\mathrm{LP}(G)$ be the greatest number of homotopy breakpoints over all
$2^{|C|}$ base profiles, with $d=\bbone$. Exactly computed:
\[
\begin{array}{lccl}
\text{family} & |C| & \mathrm{LP}(G) & \\\hline
L_n\ (\Cref{def:ladder}),\ n\le10 & n & n & \text{(all-switches also }n)\\
P_D\ (\Cref{thm:fold}),\ D\le6 & 2D & 0,2,4,6,10,12 & \\
\mathrm{RC}(k)\ (\Cref{rem:grid-per-vertex}),\ k\le10 & k & k & \\
M_n\ (\Cref{prop:modulator-family}),\ n\le6 & 2n+1 & \le2n+1 & \\
G^{\ast}\ (19\text{ vertices, two-player}) & 7 & 9 &\\
\mathrm{WD},\ \mathrm{CC},\ H_m,\ G_m & \le2 & \le3\ \text{(by }|C|)&
\end{array}
\]
($G^{\ast}$ is the $19$-vertex two-player instance of the harness, and is the
smallest example here on which the path exceeds $|C|$. $G^{\sharp}$ and
$B^{2}$ were not measured: recovering their harmonic normal form needs an
exact $130\times130$ elimination that did not finish in the time available.)
In particular the ladder, on which least-index and smallest-gap need
$2^{n}-1$ switches (\Cref{thm:ladder}), costs the homotopy exactly $n$; and
\Cref{thm:fold}'s fold, which defeats every continuation method that sweeps a
frozen payoff, costs it at most $|C|$. The reason is visible in
\Cref{fsa:def-bias}: the bias $d_v\lambda$ is the same at every level of the
fold while the fold's own scales are geometric, so all levels below $\lambda$
are flattened by the dead zone and each wakes exactly once as $\lambda$ falls.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Murty's matrix is realisable, and does not help]\label{fsa:murty}
Let $m\ge2$ and let $R_m$ be the one-player reduced game on $C=\{1,\dots,m\}$
whose base action at $i$ goes to $i-1$ with probability $1$ (and at $i=1$ to
the sinks), and whose other action at $i$ goes to $j$ with probability
$2^{j-i+1}$ for $j\le i-2$. Both matrices are strictly lower triangular,
dyadic and substochastic --- row sums $0,1,1,\dots$ and
$0,0,\tfrac12,\tfrac34,\dots,1-2^{2-m}$ --- so every profile is transient and
$R_m$ is the harmonic normal form of a stopping SSG (\Cref{lem:dyadic-row}).
Its complementarity matrix is
$(I-Q_1)(I-Q_0)^{-1}=D(I+2L)D^{-1}$ with $D=\mathrm{diag}(2^{-i})$ and $L$
strictly lower triangular all-ones: Murty's $P$-matrix, the standard
exponential example for least-index principal pivoting, occurs among simple
stochastic games. It is nevertheless \emph{not} an example here: the
parametric path of $\mathrm{LCP}(I+2L,\,-\bbone+\lambda d)$ has exactly one
breakpoint for $d=\bbone$ and for $d=(2^{i})_i$, and at most $m$ for the
reward vectors admissible in $R_m$ (exact, $m\le8$). Lemke's path and the
least-index principal pivoting path are different objects, and only the latter
is exponential on Murty's matrix.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[The path exceeds $|C|$; four exact witnesses]\label{fsa:witnesses}
Let $\mathrm{LP}^{\ast}(m)$ be the greatest number of homotopy breakpoints,
with $d=\bbone$, over stopping SSGs with $|C|=m$ and over all base profiles.
Then, witnessed by explicit \emph{one-player} stopping SSGs verified from the
game,
\[
  \mathrm{LP}^{\ast}(1)=1,\quad \mathrm{LP}^{\ast}(2)=3=2^{2}-1,\quad
  \mathrm{LP}^{\ast}(3)\ge7=2^{3}-1,\quad
  \mathrm{LP}^{\ast}(4)\ge12,\quad \mathrm{LP}^{\ast}(5)\ge18,\quad
  \mathrm{LP}^{\ast}(6)\ge22 .
\]
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=2.2em]
\item $\mathrm{HAM}_3$, on $35$ vertices ($3$ Max, $30$ average, normal-form
  denominator $2^{4}$), base profile $\beta=(1,0,0)$: the homotopy visits
  \emph{all eight} profiles,
  $100\to110\to010\to000\to001\to011\to111\to101$, a Hamiltonian path in the
  profile cube, and Lemke makes $8$ pivots. Its normal form is
  \[
  \begin{array}{l|ll}
   v & f_{v,0} & f_{v,1}\\\hline
   1 & \tfrac18+\tfrac58y_1+\tfrac18y_2 & \tfrac1{16}y_1+\tfrac{11}{16}y_3\\
   2 & \tfrac58y_1+\tfrac5{16}y_2 & \tfrac18+\tfrac1{16}y_1+\tfrac7{16}y_3\\
   3 & \tfrac14+\tfrac7{16}y_3 & \tfrac1{16}+\tfrac{15}{16}y_3
  \end{array}
  \]
  with $\val|_C=(\tfrac{11}{15},\tfrac23,1)$ and breakpoints
  $\tfrac{47}{1080},\tfrac{391}{9720},\tfrac{283}{7416},\tfrac5{144},
   \tfrac{29}{876},\tfrac{71}{2216},\tfrac7{232}$;
\item $\mathrm{PATH}_4$, on $233$ vertices ($4$ Max, $227$ average),
  $\beta=(0,0,0,0)$: the homotopy visits $13$ of the $16$ profiles,
  $0000\to0100\to1100\to1000\to1001\to1011\to1010\to1110\to1111\to0111\to
  0110\to0010\to0011$; Lemke makes $13$ pivots;
\item $\mathrm{PATH}_5$, on $232$ vertices ($5$ Max, $225$ average),
  $\beta=(0,0,0,0,1)$: the homotopy visits $19$ of the $32$ profiles; Lemke
  makes $19$ pivots;
\item $\mathrm{PATH}_6$, on $300$ vertices ($6$ Max, $292$ average),
  $\beta=(0,0,0,0,1,0)$: the homotopy visits $23$ of the $64$ profiles; Lemke
  makes $23$ pivots.
\end{enumerate}
All four were built from their dyadic normal forms by \Cref{lem:dyadic-row}
and verified \emph{from the game}: stopping by \Cref{lem:trapchar}, the normal
form recomputed from the successor lists, the value against brute force over
all profiles, and the path by two independent implementations --- the
game-side homotopy and a lexicographic Lemke tableau on $(M,q)$, whose pivot
count is one more than the number of breakpoints. In particular no bound
$\mathrm{LP}\le|C|$ and no bound $\mathrm{LP}\le2|C|$ holds.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[Exactly what is missing]\label{fsa:gap}
One statement: \emph{there are $c>0$ and stopping SSGs $G_n$ with
$|C(G_n)|=n$, a base profile and covering vector $\bbone$, on which the bias
homotopy makes at least $2^{cn}$ switches.} By \Cref{fsa:shadow} its
one-player half asks for a two-action Markov decision process whose occupancy
polytope has an exponentially long shadow in a direction selecting one
action-coordinate per state; by \Cref{fsa:one-improving} such a family would
also be an exponential lower bound for an improving single-switch rule that is
none of the seven of \Cref{prop:rules-fail} and is not all-switches.
Conversely a polynomial bound on the path would, by
\Cref{fsa:lemke-homotopy}, put \textsc{SSG-Value} in $\mathsf P$, so no
polynomial bound may be conjectured here.

The obstruction met in the search is concrete, and can be stated as a lemma.
Let an outer Max vertex $u$ be added to a block $B$ so that both of $u$'s
actions enter $B$ at the same distribution up to a positive scalar: action $x$
sends the token into $B$ with law $c_x\mu$ and pays $r_{u,x}$. Then for a
policy $(x,\tau)$ the shadow coordinates are
$\kappa=[x{=}1]+c_x\kappa_{\mathrm{in}}(\mu,\tau)$ and
$\val=r_{u,x}+c_x\val_{\mathrm{in}}(\mu,\tau)$, so each of the two copies of
the inner point set is a \emph{homothety} of it: the two copies carry
identical slope sequences $s_1>\dots>s_{k-1}$, where $k$ is the number of
vertices of the inner hull. If the copies are separated in $\kappa$ --- which
they are as soon as the $+1$ from $u$'s own occupancy dominates --- the joint
hull takes a prefix of length $p$ of one and a suffix of length $q$ of the
other, and concavity forces $s_{p-1}>s_{k-q+1}$, i.e.\ $p+q\le k+1$: the
doubling gains \emph{at most one} vertex. Doubling therefore requires the two
actions of $u$ to enter $B$ with \emph{non-proportional} weightings, so that
the second copy's slopes are compressed below the first's. That is what
$\mathrm{HAM}_3$ achieves at $m=3$; iterating it is open.
The measured growth of $\mathrm{LP}^{\ast}$ is
$1,3,7,\ge12,\ge18,\ge22$ at $m=1,\dots,6$, against the ceiling
$2^{m}-1=1,3,7,15,31,63$: the ceiling is attained at $m\le3$ and the
successive differences are $2,4,5,6,\ge4$, the last search being the least
converged. Nothing may be read into the shape of that sequence:
every search in this project that looked for a hard instance failed until the
instance was engineered, and a quadratic bound on $\mathrm{LP}^{\ast}$ would
put \textsc{SSG-Value} in $\mathsf P$, so it must not be conjectured. What the
numbers do establish is the lower bounds above; they were obtained by local
search on the \emph{transition structure} with the rewards eliminated by
\Cref{fsa:path-lp}, and by local search on the reduced game scored by the
exact path length, in both cases followed by dyadic rounding and
re-verification from the built game --- not by sampling raw games, which never
exceeded $|C|+2$.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Warm-starting the width search}\label{sec:fsa-warm}

The amortisation statement missing from \Cref{sec:width} asks that the
$O(B^{k+1})$ payoff vectors queried at a separator be answerable in total time
polynomially related to one of them. The following is the part of it that is
true; it divides the query count of \Cref{thm:qp} by a factorial and no more.

\begin{lemma}[Lipschitz warm start]\label{fsa:warm}
Let $H$ be a stopping payoff game, $X$ a set of non-terminals, and for
$\theta\in[0,1]^{X}$ let $H[\theta]$ be $H$ with $X$ retyped as terminals of
payoff $\theta$. Let $S$ be a set of non-terminals disjoint from $X$, let
$F^{\theta}_S$ be the cut map of $H[\theta]$ at $S$ (\Cref{lem:cut}) and
$y(\theta):=\val_{H[\theta]}|_S$ its unique fixed point
(\Cref{lem:cut-sign}). Then for all $\theta,\theta'\in[0,1]^{X}$, with
$\delta:=\|\theta-\theta'\|_\infty$, the box
$B:=[y(\theta)-\delta\bbone,\;y(\theta)+\delta\bbone]\cap[0,1]^{S}$
contains $y(\theta')$ and satisfies $F^{\theta'}_S(B)\subseteq B$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The value of a stopping payoff game is monotone and $1$-Lipschitz in the
vector of terminal payoffs, in the supremum norm: if
$\vartheta\le\vartheta'\le\vartheta+\delta\bbone$ then
$T_{\vartheta'}\ge T_\vartheta$ pointwise, so
$\val_{\vartheta}\le\val_{\vartheta'}$ by \Cref{lem:payoff-transfer}(c), while
$\hat x:=\val_{\vartheta}+\delta\bbone$ satisfies
$T_{\vartheta'}\hat x\le T_{\vartheta}\val_\vartheta+\delta\bbone=\hat x$ and
hence $\val_{\vartheta'}\le\hat x$ by the same; a general pair is compared
through $\vartheta\wedge\vartheta'$ and $\vartheta\vee\vartheta'$. Applied to
the terminal set $X$ this gives $\|y(\theta')-y(\theta)\|_\infty\le\delta$, so
$y(\theta')\in B$. Applied to the terminal set $X\cup S$ it gives, for
$x\in B$,
$\|\val_{H[\theta',x]}-\val_{H[\theta,y(\theta)]}\|_\infty\le
\max(\|\theta'-\theta\|_\infty,\|x-y(\theta)\|_\infty)\le\delta$; and
$\operatorname{op}_a$ is $1$-Lipschitz, so
$\|F^{\theta'}_S(x)-F^{\theta}_S(y(\theta))\|_\infty\le\delta$, while
$F^{\theta}_S(y(\theta))=y(\theta)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[The warm-started search]\label{fsa:warm-count}
Run the algorithm of \Cref{thm:qp} with each child search started, at every
query after the first, from the box of \Cref{fsa:warm} around the answer to
the previous query. It remains correct --- \Cref{thm:tarski} needs only a box
that $F$ maps into itself --- and in a decomposition all of whose separators
are single vertices the number of leaf solves drops from $(2B+4)^{L}$ to at
most $2^{L}\binom{B+L}{L}$, with $B$ the number of grid bits and $L$ the
recursion depth: the $j$-th query of a node's binary search lies within
$2^{-(j-1)}$ of the previous one, so the child's box has side at most
$2^{-(j-2)}$ and only $B-j+O(1)$ bits remain, giving
$W_\ell(r)=\sum_{j\ge r}2W_{\ell+1}(j)$ and
$W_{L-i}(r)=2^{i}\binom{B-r+i}{i}$. Since
$\binom{B+L}{L}\le(e(B+L)/L)^{L}$, the exponent improves from $L\log_2B$ to
$L(\log_2B-\log_2L+O(1))$. It does not become polynomial.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}\label{fsa:warm-remark}
This is exactly the amortisation that monotonicity and nonexpansiveness buy,
and it is not enough, for the reason behind \Cref{rem:fold-width}: each query
still needs the child solved \emph{exactly}. Solving it only to the current
box's precision does not suffice, the cut map being a contraction of rate only
$1-2^{-N}$: knowing $F$ to within $\eta$ locates its fixed point only to
within $\eta2^{N}$. What the warm start saves is the number of \emph{bits}
still to be determined, not the number of solves.
(\Cref{fsa:warm} was verified exactly on $108$ and then, at a fresh seed and
on larger games, on a further $59$ random stopping games with random $X$, $S$
and $\theta,\theta'$: no failure of containment or of invariance.)
\end{remark}
