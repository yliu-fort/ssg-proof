\begin{lemma}[What a translation does to an outmap]\label{b3:translate}
Let $s$ be an outmap of the $m$-cube, $z\in\{0,1\}^{m}$, and
$t(v):=s(v\oplus z)$. Write $g_i(v)=+$ when the $i$-edge at $v$ points from
$v_i=0$ to $v_i=1$ (a function of $v_{-i}$ alone). Then for $i\notin z$,
$g^{t}_i(v)=g_i(v\oplus z)$, and for $i\in z$,
$g^{t}_i(v)=-\,g_i(v\oplus(z\setminus\{i\}))$. In particular translation by a
single coordinate $e_j$ \emph{reverses every $j$-edge} and leaves every other
coordinate's edge direction unchanged except that it is read at the flipped
$\sigma_j$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$g^t_i(v)=+$ iff bit $i$ of $t(v|_{i=0})=s((v|_{i=0})\oplus z)$ is $1$. If
$i\notin z$ then $(v|_{i=0})\oplus z=(v\oplus z)|_{i=0}$, giving the first
clause. If $i\in z$ then $(v|_{i=0})\oplus z=(v\oplus(z\setminus\{i\}))|_{i=1}$,
and in a unique sink orientation bit $i$ of $s(w|_{i=1})$ is the complement of
bit $i$ of $s(w|_{i=0})$, whence the sign change.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The blow-up needs the parity only along the walk]\label{b3:free-parity}
Let $s$ be an acyclic unique sink orientation of the $m$-cube with sink $o$ and
height function $h$. Let $p:\{0,1\}^{m}\to\{0,1\}$ be \emph{any} function and
$z\in\{0,1\}^{m}$ \emph{any} vector. Define $B_{p,z}(s)$ on the
$(m+2)$-cube exactly as in \Cref{thm:blowup}, with inner part $s(v\oplus z)$ on
the layer $(\alpha,\beta)=(0,0)$ and $s(v)$ elsewhere, and outer part
\[
 \begin{array}{c|cc}
   (\alpha,\beta) & p(v)=0 & p(v)=1\\ \hline
   (0,0) & \emptyset & \emptyset\\
   (1,0) & \{\alpha,\beta\} & \{\alpha\}\\
   (0,1) & \{\beta\} & \{\alpha,\beta\}\\
   (1,1) & \{\alpha\} & \{\beta\}
 \end{array}
\]
Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=2.2em]
\item $B_{p,z}(s)$ is an acyclic unique sink orientation of the $(m+2)$-cube,
      with sink $(0,0,o\oplus z)$, for every $p$ and every $z$;
\item if $u$ is a vertex and $p(w)\equiv h(w)\pmod 2$ at every vertex $w$ of
      the bottom-antipodal walk of $s$ from $u$, then the bottom-antipodal
      walk of $B_{p,z}(s)$ from $(1,0,u)$ (if $h(u)$ is even) or $(0,1,u)$ (if
      $h(u)$ is odd) has length exactly $h(u)+2+h(o\oplus z)$, so
      $h^{\ast}(B_{p,z}(s))\ge h(u)+2+h(o\oplus z)$.
\end{enumerate}
\Cref{thm:blowup} is the case $p=h\bmod 2$, $z=o\oplus u$ with $h(u)$ maximal.
Taking $u$ and $o\oplus z$ both of maximal height gives height $\ge 2h^{\ast}(s)+2$
as there, but leaves $p$ free at the $2^{m}-h(u)-1$ vertices off the walk and
$z$ free among $\{o\oplus u':h(u')=h^{\ast}(s)\}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(a) Translation by $z$ is an automorphism of the cube, so $v\mapsto s(v\oplus z)$
is again an acyclic unique sink orientation, with sink $o\oplus z$. The three
verifications in the proof of \Cref{thm:blowup} use the parity only through the
following four properties of the displayed table, each of which holds for both
of its columns and hence for every $p$: the four outer sets form a unique sink
orientation of the $2$-cube (Szab\'o--Welzl on all six pairs); the layer $(0,0)$
receives $\emptyset$; each layer $L\ne(0,0)$ receives a coordinate on which $L$
has a $1$; and, at a fixed $v$, the outer arcs among the three layers
$\ne(0,0)$ form a directed path. The unique-sink argument, the acyclicity
argument and the identification of the sink are then verbatim those of
\Cref{thm:blowup}: no outer edge leaves layer $(0,0)$, so a cycle meeting it
lies in it and contradicts the acyclicity of the translate; a cycle avoiding it
projects to a closed directed walk of $s$, hence to a constant $v$, and at a
fixed $v$ a directed path carries no cycle. The sink is the unique vertex whose
outmap is empty, that is layer $(0,0)$ with $s(v\oplus z)=\emptyset$, namely
$v=o\oplus z$.

(b) Along the walk of $s$ from $u$ the height drops by one per step, so $p$
alternates on it. At $(1,0,w)$ with $p(w)=0$ the outmap is
$\{\alpha,\beta\}\cup s(w)$, so one step gives $(0,1,w\oplus s(w))$, whose
inner vertex is the next vertex of the walk and has the other parity; at
$(0,1,w')$ with $p(w')=1$ the outmap is again $\{\alpha,\beta\}\cup s(w')$ and
the step returns to layer $(1,0)$. After $h(u)$ steps the inner vertex is $o$
and the layer is $(1,0)$ in both cases. There $p(o)=0$ and $s(o)=\emptyset$
give the step to $(0,1,o)$, where the outmap is $\{\beta\}$ and the step goes
to $(0,0,o)$. Inside layer $(0,0)$ the outer part is empty and the walk is, in
the coordinates $y:=v\oplus z$, the bottom-antipodal walk of $s$ from
$y=o\oplus z$, of length $h(o\oplus z)$, ending at $y=o$, that is at the sink
$(0,0,o\oplus z)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The reversed coordinate reads the rise]\label{b3:rise-sign}
Let $G$ be a nondegenerate stopping SSG realising $B_{p,e_j}(s)$ with
$\Vmax=\{$inner$\}\cup\{\alpha,\beta\}$. For an inner strategy $v$ put
$y_v:=\val_{(0,0,v)}$, $x_v:=\val_{(1,0,v)}$ and $x'_v:=\val_{(0,1,v)}$. Then
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=2.2em]
\item $y_v\ge x_v$ and $y_v\ge x'_v$ pointwise on all of $V$, for every $v$ ---
      $\alpha$ is switchable at $(1,0,v)$ and switching it gives $(0,0,v)$,
      $\beta$ is switchable at $(0,1,v)$ and switching it gives $(0,0,v)$
      (\Cref{lem:switch});
\item with $w:=p^{j,1}-p^{j,0}$ the coefficient vector of the reversed
      coordinate's margin $\Delta_j$, one has
      $\operatorname{sign}\bigl(w\cdot(y_v-x_v)\bigr)
      =\operatorname{sign}\Delta_j(y_v)=-\operatorname{sign}\Delta_j(x_v)$ for
      every $v$;
\item consequently $w$ has entries of both signs, and the $2^{m}$ rise vectors
      $y_v-x_v\ge0$ are split by the hyperplane $w^{\perp}$ exactly according
      to the edge direction $g_j$ of $s$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(a) is \Cref{lem:switch} for the single switches $\{\alpha\}$ at $(1,0,v)$ and
$\{\beta\}$ at $(0,1,v)$, both improving because the outer part of the layer
$(1,0)$ always contains $\alpha$ and that of $(0,1)$ always contains $\beta$.
(b) By \Cref{b3:translate} the $j$-edge is reversed between
the layer $(0,0)$ and the layer $(1,0)$, so $\Delta_j(y_v)$ and $\Delta_j(x_v)$
have opposite signs; $\Delta_j$ is affine with linear part $w$, so
$\Delta_j(y_v)-\Delta_j(x_v)=w\cdot(y_v-x_v)$ has the sign of $\Delta_j(y_v)$.
(c) If $w\ge0$ then $w\cdot(y_v-x_v)\ge0$ for all $v$ by (a), so $\Delta_j(y_v)>0$
for all $v$ and $g_j$ is constant on the inner cube, which no coordinate of a
unique sink orientation of dimension $\ge2$ has. Dually for $w\le0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Checked on \Cref{prop:b2-realised}'s game with $j=\beta_1$: $w=\tfrac1{512}
(267,187,0,0,56,-460)$, the eight rises are nonnegative, and
$w\cdot(y_v-x_v)$ is positive at $v=0,1,2,4,5,6$ and negative at $v=3,7$,
matching the layer-$(0,0)$ sign of $\Delta_{\beta_1}$ at every $v$.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[A valid parity is affinely readable; the true one is not]\label{b3:parity-readable}

\begin{lemma}[The layer-$(0,0)$ two-cycle must be almost lossless]\label{b3:two-cycle}
Suppose a realisation has, as in \Cref{b3:l2-design}(i) and \Cref{b3:tb}, an
outer pair whose action-$0$ rows are $A\val(\beta)+q$ and $A'\val(\alpha)$ with
$A,A',q>0$, and suppose the inner vertices do not read the outer pair, so that
the inner values $z_v$ are fixed. Write $f,h$ for the two action-$1$ rows, both
substochastic affine (hence monotone) in $z$, and $\Theta:=q/(1-AA')$. Then the
outer rule of \Cref{b3:free-parity} holds iff, writing $F:=f-Ah-q$ and
$H:=h-A'f$, for every $v$
\[
 f(z_v)<\Theta,\qquad h(z_v)<A'\Theta,\qquad
 \max\bigl(F(z_v),H(z_v)\bigr)>0,\qquad
 F(z_v)>0\iff p(v)=1 ;
\]
$F$ and $H$ are never both positive. Consequently, for every pair of
inner strategies with $z_v\ge z_{v'}$ componentwise, $p(v)=1$ and $p(v')=0$,
\[
  1-AA'\;<\;\frac{\pi\cdot(z_v-z_{v'})}{\pi\cdot(z_{\ast}-z_{v'})}
  \;\le\;\max_{j\,:\,(z_\ast-z_{v'})_j>0}\frac{(z_v-z_{v'})_j}{(z_{\ast}-z_{v'})_j},
\]
where $\pi\ge0$ is the coefficient vector of $f$ and $z_{\ast}=\val$ is the
value vector of the optimal strategy, which dominates all the others; if the
denominator vanishes the design is infeasible. On the level-two configuration
and the $p$ of \Cref{b3:parity-readable} the pair $(v,v')=(20,28)$ forces
$1-AA'<\tfrac{78974227846783}{2781927489443544}=0.028389$ and the pair
$(16,8)$ forces $1-AA'<0.031184$; \Cref{prop:b2-realised} has
$1-AA'=3313/131072=0.025276$ --- inside the bound by ten per cent --- and
\Cref{b3:tb} has $1-AA'=4093/1048576=0.003903$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
On the layer $(0,0)$ the two action-$0$ rows close on each other, so
$\val(\alpha)=\Theta$ and $\val(\beta)=A'\Theta$ there; on the layer $(1,0)$,
$\val(\alpha)=f$, $\val(\beta)=A'f$; on $(0,1)$, $\val(\beta)=h$,
$\val(\alpha)=Ah+q$; on $(1,1)$, $\val(\alpha)=f$ and $\val(\beta)=h$.
Substituting into $\Delta_\alpha=f-A\val(\beta)-q$ and
$\Delta_\beta=h-A'\val(\alpha)$ gives $\Delta_\alpha=f-\Theta$ on the layer
$(0,0)$ and $\Delta_\alpha=(1-AA')f-q$ on the layer $(1,0)$ --- using
$\Theta=AA'\Theta+q$ --- both negative exactly when $f<\Theta$, and
$\Delta_\alpha=F$ on both layers with $\sigma_\beta=1$, the same expression in
both, as the rule requires; dually $\Delta_\beta=h-A'\Theta$ on $(0,0)$ and
$(1-AA')h-A'q$ on $(0,1)$, both negative exactly when $h<A'\Theta$, and
$\Delta_\beta=H$ on both layers with $\sigma_\alpha=1$. Since
$AH+F=(1-AA')f-q<0$ under the first condition, $F$ and $H$ are never both
positive. For the inequality: $F(z_v)>0$ gives
$f(z_v)>Ah(z_v)+q\ge Ah(z_{v'})+q>AA'f(z_{v'})+q$ by monotonicity of $h$ and
$H(z_{v'})>0$; writing $f(z_v)-f(z_{v'})=\pi\cdot(z_v-z_{v'})$ and
$\Theta-f(z_{v'})\ge f(z_{\ast})-f(z_{v'})=\pi\cdot(z_{\ast}-z_{v'})$ (using
$f(z_{\ast})<\Theta$) turns it into the displayed bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[The translation must cross a fixed threshold]\label{b3:theta-floor}
Let $G$ be a nondegenerate stopping SSG realising $B_{p,e_{j}}(s)$, and suppose
the translated coordinate $j$ and one further Max vertex $j'$ carry the
two-cycle rows of \Cref{b3:l2-design}(i): action $0$ of $j$ is
$A\val(j')+q$ and action $0$ of $j'$ is $A'\val(j)$, with $A,A',q>0$ and no
other targets. Then at every Max strategy $\sigma$ with
$\sigma_{j}=\sigma_{j'}=0$ --- in every one of the four layers ---
\[
  \val_\sigma(j)=\Theta:=\frac{q}{1-AA'},\qquad \val_\sigma(j')=A'\Theta ,
\]
independently of all the other values. Writing $\Psi$ for the action-$1$ row of
$j$, the reversal of the $j$-edges on the layer $(0,0)$ (\Cref{b3:translate})
forces
\[
 \Psi(\val_{(0,0,v)})>\Theta \quad\text{and}\quad \Psi(\val_{(L,v)})<\Theta
 \quad (L\ne(0,0))
\]
for every one of the $2^{m-2}$ inner strategies $v$ with $v_j=v_{j'}=0$ at
which $g_j=-$ in $s$, and the two inequalities exchanged where $g_j=+$. Since
$\Psi$ is substochastic, $\Psi>\Theta$ needs some vertex read by $\Psi$ to have
value $>\Theta$: the layer-$(0,0)$ rise of \Cref{b3:rise-sign} must carry the
readout across an \emph{absolute} threshold fixed by the two rows of the
previous level's outer pair, not merely reorder the values. For the published
level-two realisation this threshold is $\Theta=\tfrac{2048}{3313}=0.6182$
while its own inner values never exceed $0.5538$; so in a level-three
realisation built on those rows the inner vertices must read the new outer pair
$(\alpha_3,\beta_3)$ hard enough to lift the readout of $\alpha_2$ by at least
$0.065$ on the layer $(0,0)$ and by nothing at all on the other three.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
On the layer of $\sigma$ the two action-$0$ rows close on each other, so
$\val_\sigma(j)=A\val_\sigma(j')+q$ and $\val_\sigma(j')=A'\val_\sigma(j)$,
whose unique solution is the displayed pair ($AA'<1$ because the rows are
substochastic and the game is stopping). The margin of $j$ at such a $\sigma$
is $\Psi(\val_\sigma)-\Theta$, and its sign is $g_j$, which
\Cref{b3:translate} reverses between the layer $(0,0)$ and the other three.
The last clause is $|p|_1+q\le1$ and $\val\le1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[A level splits into three systems with one set of rows]\label{b3:decompose}
Let $G$ be a stopping SSG with $\Vmax=I\cup\{\alpha,\beta\}$, $|I|=m$, and
$\Vmin=Y$, whose rows satisfy: action $0$ of $\alpha$ reads only $\beta$ and
the sinks; action $0$ of $\beta$ reads only $\alpha$ and the sinks; action $1$
of $\beta$ does not read $\alpha$; and no vertex of $I\cup Y$ reads $\alpha$.
Write $\Psi_\alpha,\Psi_\beta$ for the action-$1$ rows of $\alpha,\beta$, and
$A,A',q$ as in \Cref{b3:two-cycle}. Then for every Max strategy $(L,v)$ the
values on $I\cup Y$ depend on the layer $L$ only through the three classes
$\{(0,0)\}$, $\{(1,0)\}$, $\{(0,1),(1,1)\}$, and on each class they are the
values of the $m$-Max stopping SSG obtained from the rows of $I\cup Y$ by
substituting for $\val(\beta)$, respectively,
\[
  A'\Theta \ \ (\text{a constant}),\qquad A'\Psi_\alpha(\cdot),\qquad
  \Psi_\beta(\cdot).
\]
Consequently a realisation of $B_{p,z}(s)$ by such a $G$ is exactly: one family
of rows on $I\cup Y$ which, under the constant substitution, realises
$s\circ(\oplus z)$ and, under each of the other two substitutions, realises
$s$; together with the outer conditions on $\alpha,\beta$. The game of
\Cref{prop:b2-realised} satisfies all four hypotheses, and its inner value
vectors on the layers $(0,1)$ and $(1,1)$ are indeed equal, strategy by
strategy.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
On the layer $(0,0)$ the two action-$0$ rows close on each other
(\Cref{b3:theta-floor}), giving $\val(\beta)=A'\Theta$ whatever the rest; on
$(1,0)$, $\val(\alpha)=\Psi_\alpha$ and $\val(\beta)=A'\val(\alpha)$; on
$(0,1)$ and $(1,1)$, $\val(\beta)=\Psi_\beta$, which does not involve
$\val(\alpha)$. Since no vertex of $I\cup Y$ reads $\alpha$, the equations for
$I\cup Y$ close once $\val(\beta)$ is substituted, and the substituted system
is a stopping SSG's fixed-point system, whose solution is unique
(\Cref{thm:contraction}); the improvement outmap of $G$ restricted to a layer
is that of the substituted system.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The rise has one direction per selected profile]\label{b3:rise-direction}
Assume the hypotheses of \Cref{b3:decompose} and let $v$ be an inner strategy
at which one Min strategy is a best response both at $(0,0,v)$ and at
$(0,1,v)$. Let $M_v$ be the inner part and $b_v\ge0$ the vector of
$\beta$-slot weights of the rows selected at $v$. Then
\[
  \val_{(0,0,v)}|_{I\cup Y}-\val_{(0,1,v)}|_{I\cup Y}
  \;=\;(I-M_v)^{-1}b_v\cdot\bigl(\theta-\Psi_\beta(\val_{(0,1,v)})\bigr),
\]
a \emph{nonnegative multiple of the single vector} $(I-M_v)^{-1}b_v\ge0$. Hence
for the translated coordinate $j$, with $w=p^{j,1}-p^{j,0}$ restricted to
$I\cup Y$,
\[
  \operatorname{sign}\bigl(w\cdot(\val_{(0,0,v)}-\val_{(0,1,v)})\bigr)
  =\operatorname{sign}\bigl(w\cdot(I-M_v)^{-1}b_v\bigr),
\]
the sign of the transfer impedance from the new $\beta$ to the margin of $j$
under the profile selected at $v$; by \Cref{b3:rise-sign} it must follow $g_j$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Both value vectors solve $y=M_vy+c_v+b_v\cdot s$ with the same $M_v,c_v,b_v$,
with $s=\theta$ on the layer $(0,0)$ and $s=\Psi_\beta(\val_{(0,1,v)})$ on the
layer $(0,1)$ --- the latter because $\val_{(0,1,v)}(\beta)=\Psi_\beta$
evaluated at that same vector. Subtracting and inverting $I-M_v$, which is
invertible with nonnegative inverse because the game is stopping, gives the
displayed identity; $\theta\ge\Psi_\beta(\val_{(0,1,v)})$ by
\Cref{b3:rise-sign}(a) applied to the vertex $\beta$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[measured: this is what the Min vertex is for]\label{b3:min-role}
On \Cref{prop:b2-realised}'s game, with $I\cup Y=\{x,\alpha_1,\beta_1,c_{\min}\}$,
the slot $\beta_2$, $j=\beta_1$ and $\theta=\tfrac{2036}{3313}$, the identity of
\Cref{b3:rise-direction} holds \emph{exactly} at the six inner strategies
$0,1,2,4,5,6$ and fails at $3$ and $7$ --- precisely the two at which the Min
vertex changes its best response between the layer $(0,0)$ (action $0$) and the
other three (action $1$), and precisely the two at which the reversal of
$\Delta_{\beta_1}$ goes in the direction $+\to-$. At the other six,
$w\cdot(I-M_v)^{-1}b_v>0$ carries the easy direction $-\to+$. So in that
realisation the Min vertex is not a parity reader but a \emph{profile
switch}: it changes $M_v$ between the two layers exactly where a fixed rise
direction could not reverse the margin.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The residue of the search, localised]\label{b3:residue}
Run on the reduction of \Cref{b3:decompose} with $z=e_{\alpha_2}$ --- twelve
independent sequential-linear-programming runs over the $104$ parameters ---
the search brings the $320$ constraints down to $10$ wrong, and every one of
the six best runs has the same six in its residue: the coordinate
$\alpha_2$ in the constant-substituted system at the inner strategies
$17,19,23,25,27,31$. Those six are exactly $\{g^{B^{2}}_{\alpha_2}=+\}$, that is
$\{\sigma_{\beta_2}=1\ \text{and}\ h_{B^{1}}(\sigma_x,\sigma_{\alpha_1},
\sigma_{\beta_1})\ \text{odd}\}$: the level-two parity readout itself. The
$26$ reversals in the direction $-\to+$ are found; the six in the direction
$+\to-$ are not. At the best point $\Psi_\beta$ has collapsed to the zero map
and all the slot weights are below $0.05$: the search settles on the
\emph{pure boost}, which by \Cref{b3:rise-direction} can reverse margins in one
direction only. No run found a configuration of the kind the level-two game
uses (\Cref{b3:min-role}). Whether that is possible at level three is the open
point; nothing here excludes it.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The gap]\label{b3:gap}
The construction of \Cref{b3:tb} realises the blow-up's outer machinery ---
two Max vertices, no new Min vertex, one further bit of precision, $54$ further
average vertices --- but with the trivial translation $z=0$, so its height is
$h^{\ast}(B^{2})+2=12$ and not $2h^{\ast}(B^{2})+2=22$. What is missing is
exactly the layer-$(0,0)$ translation: by \Cref{b3:translate} the coordinate
$\alpha_2$ must have all $32$ of its margins reversed on that layer while the
other four inner coordinates are read at the flipped $\sigma_{\alpha_2}$, which
is $84$ of the $896$ sign constraints for $z=8$ (respectively $64$ for $z=24$,
both of height $22$). A sequential-linear-programming search over the
$2n(n+1)$ entries of the normal form, warm-started at \Cref{b3:tb} and at
random, with $n=8,9,10$ and every admissible $z$, left between $30$ and $76$
constraints unsatisfied; in the best runs $28$ of the $30$ residual failures
lie in the layer $(0,0)$, on the coordinates $\beta_1,\alpha_2,\beta_2$. No
obstruction is proved: \Cref{b3:rise-sign,b3:two-cycle,b3:theta-floor,%
b3:rise-direction} are necessary conditions that the level-two realisation
satisfies and that nothing here shows to be unsatisfiable at level three.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The cost of a level, as far as it is established]\label{b3:cost}
\[
\begin{array}{l|c|c|c|c|c|c}
 & \dim & h & |\Vmax| & |\Vmin| & \text{denominator} & N\\ \hline
 B^{1} & 3 & 4 & 3 & 0 & 2^{6} & 58\ \text{(re-verified here; $39$ in \Cref{prop:m3-realised})}\\
 B^{2} & 5 & 10 & 5 & 1 & 2^{9} & 138\ \text{(\Cref{prop:b2-realised})}\\
 B_{p,0}(B^{2}) & 7 & 12 & 7 & 1 & 2^{10} & 194\ \text{(\Cref{b3:tb})}\\
 B_{p,8}(B^{2}) & 7 & 22 & \text{---} & \text{---} & \text{---} & \text{not realised}
\end{array}
\]
The step $B^{1}\to B^{2}$ cost $+2$ Max, $+1$ Min, $+3$ bits, $+80$ vertices.
The outer half of the step $B^{2}\to B_{p,0}(B^{2})$ cost $+2$ Max, $+0$ Min,
$+1$ bit, $+56$ vertices --- additive, as \Cref{rem:blowup-realise} asks --- but
it buys only $h+2$, not $2h+2$. Whether the translation can be bought at
additive cost is exactly what remains open; the two data points $B^{1},B^{2}$
are all the evidence there is, and they are consistent with both an additive
and a multiplicative law.
\end{remark}
